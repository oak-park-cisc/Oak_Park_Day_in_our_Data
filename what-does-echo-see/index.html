What ECHO sees

E.C.H.O. (Engaging Community for Healthy Outcomes) is the Village of Oak Park's care-coordination program. A program manager and two care coordinators follow up after police, fire, or Village contact, take walk-ins and calls, and connect residents to services. The team works Monday to Friday and does not respond to mental health crises.

Each count below is one logged service, not one person. Someone helped three times counts three times, so read these numbers as the team's workload, not how many residents it reached.

Services, not referrals

The dashboard counts 874 services for 2025, while the Village Board was told about 702 referrals. One referral may produce more than one logged service; the ECHO team has not yet said.

September 2025 was likely a one-time batch

It is about double the months around it, and nearly all of the extra came from Police and Fire referrals. Residents' own contacts and reported crime stayed flat, and October fell straight back. Our best guess is a one-time batch of Police and Fire follow-ups, or a catch-up in logging them, rather than a rise in need. The ECHO team has not confirmed this.

Who connected people to ECHO

Every service is logged with the source that brought the resident to ECHO. These are the same services as the other tab, sorted by where they came from instead of what they were for. They are counts of services, not referrals: one referral may lead to more than one service.

    Police and Fire drove the September 2025 spike

    Police (76) and Fire (37) roughly doubled that month while resident contacts stayed flat. Whether it reflects a real rise, a batch of follow-ups, or catch-up data entry is an open question for the ECHO team.

    Community Engagement is ECHO itself

    It is the team's own outreach, such as street outreach and district cleanings, not an outside referrer. That is why most of it is Unhoused Resident work.

    Some cells are hidden for privacy

    In the breakdown of what each source sent, small counts and the cells that would reveal them are hidden. Hidden means unavailable. It is not zero or "under 5," and nothing here estimates it.

          Limits of this data

          • Counts only. The source has no names, ages, addresses, or notes, so nothing here can be mapped or traced to a person.
          • "By kind of need" uses the dashboard's month-by-service table. "By referral source" uses its month-by-referral-source table and its referral-source-by-service table. "By weekday" uses its weekday-by-service table. These tables count the same services in different ways, so they are never added together.
          • The team works weekdays, so the timing of these counts reflects its schedule, not when needs arise.
          • Category labels have no official definitions. The descriptions here are pieced together from Village Board presentations and may not match how staff assign categories.
          • "Community context" shows reported crimes and calls for service for the whole Village. The decline in both began before ECHO, and crime is a weak measure of ECHO's mostly non-criminal work, so the tab draws no conclusions about ECHO's effect.
