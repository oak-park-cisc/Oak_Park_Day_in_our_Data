# What does ECHO see?

**The question:** What kinds of need does Oak Park's non-police response team actually encounter, month by month, and which public and nonprofit services stand behind each kind of need, or don't?

**Builds on:** project idea 15, [ECHO Community Needs and Resource Gaps](../project-ideas.md#15-echo-community-needs-and-resource-gaps).

**Why it matters:** E.C.H.O. (Engaging Community for Healthy Outcomes) is the Village's care-coordination and unarmed-response program, run by the Neighborhood Services Department since February 2025. Care Coordinators follow up on 911 calls that need a social worker more than a squad car, and Community Service Officers take low-risk calls. In its first 19 months the team logged 1,598 services. Four categories make up 78 percent of them: unhoused residents (436), behavioral health (288), senior services (269), and housing (250). Referrals come from police (467), residents themselves (450), and the fire department (286), and each source brings a different kind of need: 55 percent of fire referrals are senior services, a third of police referrals are behavioral health, and residents who call in mostly need housing or are unhoused. The dashboard shows these numbers one chart at a time. Nobody has put the trend next to a list of who in town can take the handoff, which is what the Board of Health, the Aging in Communities Commission, and the ECHO team itself would use.

## The data

- Aggregate counts, already deidentified: `data/echo-activity-oak-park.csv`, 494 rows, one row per cell of five small tables stacked in long format, February 2025 to September 2026 (September is partial). Column `breakdown` says which table a row belongs to: `service_by_month` (170 rows, month by service category), `referral_by_month` (128 rows, month by referral source), `service_by_weekday`, `service_by_time_block` (four-hour blocks of the referral timestamp), and `referral_by_service`. The other columns (`month`, `weekday`, `time_block`, `referral_source`, `service`) are filled only where they apply; `count` is the number of logged services. In the three finer tables any cell under 5 is written `<5`, and where one hidden cell could be recovered from a total, a second cell in that row or column is hidden too. Eleven service categories: Unhoused Resident, Behavioral Health, Senior Services, Housing, Youth/Family Services, Other, Financial Support, Domestic Violence, Medical Support, Food Services, and `(blank)` (25 services since July 2026 with no category). Eight referral sources: Police Department, Resident Contact, Fire Department, Community Engagement, Village of Oak Park Departments, Community Partner, Business, Emergency Housing.

## First win (15 minutes)

Open the CSV, filter `breakdown` = service_by_month, and pivot `service` against `count`: the top four categories and their share of 1,598 is the first slide. Then pivot `month` against `count` for the trend: 25 services in February 2025, a spike to 178 in September 2025, and a steady 70 to 120 a month since. Two pivots, two charts, done.

## The build (by 2:15)

Two halves on one page. Left half, what ECHO sees: a stacked monthly chart by category, plus the smaller cuts the CSV already holds. Watch for the stories inside it: unhoused-resident services rose from single digits a month in spring 2025 to 36 to 45 a month in summer 2026, while senior services fell from 24 a month to single digits over the same period; 97 percent of referrals land on weekdays and almost none on weekends, which is the team's schedule, not the community's need; `referral_by_service` shows that the fire department mostly hands over seniors and the police mostly hand over behavioral health and youth cases. Right half, who can take the handoff: a table with one row per top category and, for each, the public or nonprofit services in Oak Park that match it, what they offer, hours, and how to reach them, built from the Village and Township pages above. The demo is the join: "ECHO logged 436 unhoused-resident services; here is the entire local shelter and housing capacity that exists to receive them." Where a category has heavy volume and a thin right-hand column, that is a resource gap, and it is the finding.

## Stretch

Put ECHO next to the crime file. Aggregate `data/crime-incidents-oak-park.csv` to monthly counts by `crime_against` for February 2025 to August 2026 (about 4,200 incidents, 228 a month, three quarters of them property crime) and chart ECHO's 83 services a month beside it: for every 2.7 reported offenses, one call became a social-service referral instead. Or ask what the September 2025 spike was (new staff, a new intake form, or a real surge) by reading the Village Board packets from that fall. Or turn the resource table into a plain-language "who to call" card for each category, checked against the ECHO team's own referral list.

## No-code roles

- Resource directory researchers: two or three people who read the Village, Township, library, and agency pages and fill in the right-hand table. This is most of the project.
- A plain-language writer: the categories are internal labels; "Unhoused Resident" and "Housing" need one sentence each that a neighbor understands, and the demo needs its four findings in words.
- Someone who has used or worked in these services: to say which handoffs actually work, which have waitlists, and what the data does not capture.
- Demo owner: "this many services, these four needs, this is who exists to meet them, this is where the shelf is empty."

## Handle with care

This program serves people in crisis. The CSV holds counts only, and the cross-tabs finer than month by category already have small cells suppressed; do not try to undo that, combine tables to back out a hidden cell, or estimate anything about an individual. There is no geography in the data and none should be added: no mapping, no guessing which block a category comes from, nothing below the Village level (or police zone, if the Village ever publishes that). Do not pull the dashboard's row-level download for this project; the aggregates are enough. Anything the team publishes goes to the Board of Health and the ECHO team (echo@oak-park.us) for review before it is posted anywhere public.
