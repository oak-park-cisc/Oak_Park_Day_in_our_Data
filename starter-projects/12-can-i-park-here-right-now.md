# Can I park here right now?

**The question:** Give someone an Oak Park address, a day, and a time, and tell them in plain language whether they can park there, for how long, with what permit or pass, and where the nearest lot, garage, and EV charger are.

**Builds on:** project idea 3, [Can I Park Here Right Now?](../project-ideas.md#3-can-i-park-here-right-now)

**Why it matters:** Oak Park bans parking on every street from 2:30 to 6 a.m., every night, unless you hold a zone permit or buy an overnight pass, and the daytime rules change block by block: 3-hour limits, 2-hour limits, no parking 8 to 10 a.m. weekdays, resident-permit-only, school loading zones. The Village publishes these rules as a PDF map, a handful of web pages, 17 zone maps, and the signs themselves. Nobody has put them behind a single "type your address" box. The Village's own GIS holds 1,016 daytime restriction segments and 390 overnight-permit segments, so the pieces exist. This is an informational prototype, not a legal determination; the sign on the pole always wins, and the demo should say so on screen.

## The data

- On-street restrictions, permit zones, lots, and garages: `data/parking-restrictions-oak-park.geojson`, 1,532 polygons from the Village's Parking Restriction Areas layer, tagged `restriction_group`: 1,016 `daytime_on_street` curb segments, 390 `overnight_permit_street` segments (the blocks where a Y or Z zone permit allows overnight parking), 25 `permit_zone` areas (zones Y1 to Y9, Z1 to Z7, Z9 and their extended areas, with `enforcement_times` of 11pm - 6am for most, 9pm - 10am for Y9, 9pm - 8am for Z3), 97 `lot` and 4 `garage` footprints. The daytime segments carry a category in `parking_area_name` (Time Limits 620, No Parking Anytime 143, Time Restrictions 133, Resident Permit Parking 95, Non-Resident Permit Parking 18, PDOP Staff Permit Parking 7) and the sign wording in `location_description` on 994 of them (`3HR 8A-8P`, `2HR 9A-5P M-F`, `N/P 8-10 M-F`, `NPAT`, `RPP 6A-4P M-F`; 128 distinct strings). The structured fields `classification`, `days_of_enforcement` (DAILY, WEEKDAY, MON-SAT, SCHOOL, ALL), and `duration_restriction` (15MIN to 4HOURS) are filled on most segments, but `enforcement_times` is blank on nearly every daytime segment: the hours are only in the sign text.
  Live: VOP MapServer layer 42, `https://utility.arcgis.com/usrsvcs/servers/4cff1aaefa364b57b8c70d5c606f2088/rest/services/VOP/AGOL_VOP_Project/MapServer/42/query?where=1%3D1&outFields=*&outSR=4326&f=geojson`, the layer behind the Village's [Daytime Parking Restrictions Map](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=612c713946094edebcdbefb9efc298c6) (filter `production_notes` = Daytime Parking Restrictions), [Overnight Parking Restrictions Map](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=8fae1fbfc3434fb5b30a6e2272e0f64c), and the public-lot layer of [Modes of Transportation](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=175b26ace9b74ae899803e3e34893570).
- Where an overnight pass is not valid: `data/parking-overnight-ban-oak-park.geojson`, 431 features, `ban_type` = on_street (327 street segments: the major routes, metered blocks, and paid permit zones the pass does not cover) or lot (104 lot, enclave, garage, and lot-adjacent street polygons named by lot number). 188 of the street segments are marked `status` = Migrated with every rule field blank; the other 139 say NOPARKING, DAILY, Overnight.
  Live: layers 9 and 10 on the same MapServer (swap `/42/` for `/9/` or `/10/`), behind the printed [Overnight Parking Map](https://oak-park-open-data-portal-v2-oakparkil.hub.arcgis.com/documents/f9735ab3100c45de87045b2f95a4ea11), the [No Overnight Passes Map](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=5347005a5f2a4f21a8029a65be91e81d), and the GIS Consortium's [overnight pass web map](https://www.gisconsortium.org/WebApps/CommunityPortalWebMaps/VOP/OvernightParkingBan/index.html).
- Lots, garages, chargers, car share: `data/parking-facilities-oak-park.csv`, 124 rows, one per facility with `facility_type` (4 garage, 97 lot, 11 ev_charger at six sites, 3 ev_charger_village_vehicles_only, 9 car_share), `name`, `address` (93 of the 101 lots and garages), `latitude`/`longitude` (centroid), `ownership`, `permit_types` (Day, Night, 24-hour, Monthly), `payment_device` (METER on 29 lots, PAYSTATION at the garages), `on_overnight_ban_map`, and `guideline_url`. Capacity (`max_parking_spaces`) is present on 5 rows and `accessible_space_count` on none: the Village's layer has fields for accessible spaces but they are empty everywhere.
  Live: layer 42 filtered to `PARKINGAREATYPE IN ('LOT','GARAGE')`, layer 4 (EV charging), layer 36 (Village-only chargers), layer 3 (car share).
- Address lookup: `data/streets-oak-park.geojson`, Village centerlines with address ranges on each side. Find the segment whose `street_name` and range contain the address, take its midpoint, and pull every restriction polygon within about 20 meters.
- The rules themselves, from the Village's parking pages (they block scripts, so read them in a browser): [Parking Guidelines & Restrictions](https://www.oak-park.us/Services-Parking/Parking-Mobility-Services/Parking-Guidelines-Restrictions) (the 2:30 to 6 a.m. ban, "except when allowed by permit or overnight pass"; the permit exception applies in designated multifamily areas but not "in front of churches, schools and parks and on blocks where housing is more than 50 percent single family"; Village garages and pay-by-plate spaces are free on Sundays), [Parking Passes](https://www.oak-park.us/Services-Parking/Parking-Mobility-Services/Parking-Passes) (first three overnight passes a month free, then $7; 10 a month November to March, 15 a month April to October; not valid on major routes, by meters, or in paid permit zones), [Parking Permits](https://www.oak-park.us/Services-Parking/Parking-Mobility-Services/Parking-Permits) (overnight zone permits run 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. and "do not override daytime parking restrictions"; lot permits are Day 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., Night 6 p.m. to 8 a.m. weekdays and all weekend, or 24-hour), [Permit Maps & Zone Guidelines](https://www.oak-park.us/Services-Parking/Parking-Mobility-Services/Parking-Permit-Maps-Zone-Guidelines) (a PDF per zone and per lot), and [Snow Emergency Parking](https://www.oak-park.us/Services-Parking/Parking-Mobility-Services/Seasonal-Parking/Snow-Emergency-Parking) (two inches of snow: no parking on 16 named snow routes, odd/even sides elsewhere from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m.). The Village's own [daytime restrictions PDF map](https://www.oak-park.us/files/assets/oakpark/v/4/parking/parking-maps/daytime-parking-restrictions-map.pdf) is the picture of the daytime layer.

What the Village does not publish as data: meter and pay-station hours and rates, accessible-space locations, street cleaning schedules, and the daytime hours as structured fields. The `guideline_url` links in the lot rows point at the Village's old site paths and may not resolve; the Permit Maps page has the current PDFs.

## First win (15 minutes)

Pick five addresses: someone's home, the Main Library (834 Lake St), a school (Oak Park and River Forest High School, 201 N Scoville Ave), a restaurant block on Lake St, and the Oak Park Green Line station (South Blvd at Oak Park Ave). For each, write the rule for 10 a.m. Tuesday and 3 a.m. Saturday from the two GeoJSON files (load them in geojson.io, QGIS, or kepler.gl and click the block) and the Village pages. Three of the five will not resolve from the data alone. On the library block the daytime layer has nothing, the Y1 zone polygon touches it, and the overnight file says a pass is not valid there. The 200 block of N Scoville reads `1H 9A-2P M-F` with resident permit and `N/P BUS LZ 7A-9A 2P-4P M-F`. The 1000 block of Lake St and the Metra block of North Blvd appear only in the no-pass file. Write down which answers came from data, which from a web page, and which you would have to walk over and read off a sign. That list is the opening slide.

## The build (by 2:15)

An address-plus-day-plus-time lookup on one web page (or a spreadsheet form, if nobody wants to code) that returns three sentences: overnight (the 2:30 to 6 a.m. ban, whether a pass is valid here from `parking-overnight-ban-oak-park.geojson`, and which zone permit would work from `permit_zone`), daytime (the sign text and category of every `daytime_on_street` segment within 20 meters of the centerline midpoint, translated into words, with the time you typed checked against the hours parsed from the sign text), and where else to park (nearest lot, garage, and charger from `parking-facilities-oak-park.csv` with distance and permit or pay type). Where no daytime segment is near the address, the page says "the Village's data has no daytime restriction recorded for this block; check the sign," not "no restrictions." Of the 2,486 addressed street blocks in the centerline file, 1,362 have a daytime segment within 20 meters of the midpoint, 474 have an overnight-permit segment, and 1,036 have neither, so that message will show up a lot. A Leaflet map with the block colored by category and the facilities as pins is the visual.

## Stretch

- A printable one-page summary for an address: the three sentences, the zone map link, the pass phone number and app from the Passes page, and the disclaimer.
- Plain-language and accessibility testing: give the page to someone who has never parked in Oak Park and to a screen reader user, and rewrite until both can answer "can I leave the car here until Sunday morning."
- Spanish. The sign abbreviations (`N/P`, `L/Z`, `S/D`, `RPP`, `NPAT`) need a glossary in both languages anyway.
- Snow mode: a toggle that adds the snow-route ban and odd/even rule from the Village's page, with the 16 named routes matched against `street_name`.
- Coverage map: the 1,036 blocks with no restriction segment near them, so the Village can see where its own layer is silent.

## No-code roles

- Sign surveyors: walk three blocks near the venue, photograph every parking sign, and transcribe them into a sheet with block, side, and wording. Compare with `location_description` for the same block. Where the layer is blank or wrong, you have made the missing data.
- Plain-language writers: turn each of the 128 sign strings and the six categories into one sentence a visitor understands, and write the "we do not know" sentence.
- Someone who has gotten a ticket: tell the team what the ticket said and where, and test whether the prototype would have warned you.
- Demo owner: "this address, this time, this answer, and here is what the data could not tell us."

## Claude tips

Paste the 128 distinct `location_description` strings and ask: "parse each into start time, end time, days, limit in minutes, and rule type (no parking, time limit, permit only, loading zone); flag the ones you cannot parse." Ask for a Python function that takes a house number and street, finds the centerline segment in `streets-oak-park.geojson` whose address range contains it, returns the midpoint, and lists every polygon in `parking-restrictions-oak-park.geojson` within 20 meters, no libraries. Ask for a single-file Leaflet page that loads both GeoJSON files and the facilities CSV, colors daytime segments by `parking_area_name`, and shows the sign text on click. For the time check, ask it to write the "is 3 a.m. Saturday inside 2:30 to 6 a.m. daily" and "is 10 a.m. Tuesday inside 8A-8P M-F" logic and to test it with the five first-win addresses.
