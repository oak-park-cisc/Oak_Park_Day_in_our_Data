# Build the Oak Park transit dashboard

**The question:** Can one page show every way to get around Oak Park without a car, across every provider, with what is running right now?

**Why it matters:** Six operators move people in and out of Oak Park: CTA trains on two lines, CTA buses, Pace buses, Metra, Pace's ADA paratransit, and the Township's own lift-equipped buses for seniors and people with disabilities. Each publishes its schedule, its real-time feed, its accessibility information, and its ridership in a different place, in a different format, behind a different key. Nobody has put them on one map. Village staff have asked for exactly this, an integrated view of transit in Oak Park, and what this table builds at the showcase goes back to them. The stakes are not abstract: only one of the five CTA stations inside the Village has an elevator, weekday entries at those five stations are running at about 53 percent of 2019, and Pace's 2026 network overhaul just changed which buses come down Harlem, Lake, and North.

## The data

Start with what is cached, then wire in the live feeds provider by provider.

- Every stop and station, one row each: `data/transit-stops-oak-park.csv`, 225 rows, CTA and Pace bus stops plus CTA and Metra rail stations in and just across the Village line (`in_oak_park` = Y for 147). The columns you need here are `agency`, `stop_type`, `stop_id`, `stop_name`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `routes` (the CTA routes are 20, 66, 70, 86, 90, 91, 126, Blue Line, Green Line; the Pace routes are 307, 309, 311, 313, 314, 315, 318; Metra is UP-W), `weekday_trips`, `wheelchair_boarding` (CTA rail only: 1 accessible, 2 not), `cmap_sheltered_2024`, and Pace boardings `apc_total`. Brief 06 documents the rest.
- Ridership over time: `data/cta-ridership-oak-park.csv`, 30,359 rows from the City of Chicago Data Portal. `series` = `rail_station_daily` is one row per day since 2015 for the seven 'L' stations in the stops file (`id` is the CTA station id, the same as `stop_id` there; `daytype` W weekday, A Saturday, U Sunday or holiday; `rides` is turnstile entries). `series` = `bus_route_monthly` is one row per month per route for the seven CTA bus routes (`avg_weekday_rides`, `avg_saturday_rides`, `avg_sunday_holiday_rides`, `month_total`). Both run through June 2026. Regenerate with `python3 data/scripts/fetch_cta_ridership_oak_park.py --since 2001` for the full history back to January 2001.
  Live: [L Station Entries, Daily Totals](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/CTA-Ridership-L-Station-Entries-Daily-Totals/5neh-572f) (`https://data.cityofchicago.org/resource/5neh-572f.json`) and [Bus Routes, Monthly Day-Type Averages](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/CTA-Ridership-Bus-Routes-Monthly-Day-Type-Averages/bynn-gwxy) (`https://data.cityofchicago.org/resource/bynn-gwxy.json`). The station names there are `Harlem-Lake`, `Oak Park-Lake`, `Ridgeland`, `Austin-Lake`, `Oak Park-Forest Park`, `Austin-Forest Park`, `Harlem-Forest Park`. CTA publishes no stop-level bus ridership.
- Who lives near each stop: `data/social-vulnerability-oak-park.geojson`, 53 block groups scored 1 to 5 on `Senior_Index`, `Disability_Index`, `LackOfVehicle_Index` and twelve more. Brief 06 has the live URL and the caveats.
- Bikeways: the Village's Oak Park Bikeways (July 2025) layer, already in [brief 04](04-can-a-kid-bike-to-school-safely.md), GeoJSON at `https://services5.arcgis.com/aymthbPDQOcCnuwg/arcgis/rest/services/Oak_Park_Bikeways_July_2025/FeatureServer/0/query?where=1%3D1&outFields=*&outSR=4326&f=geojson`.

The providers, with where each thing lives. Every link below was checked on September 8, 2026.

**CTA.** Green Line: Harlem/Lake, Oak Park, Ridgeland (and Austin, across the line). Blue Line: Harlem, Oak Park, Austin. Bus routes 90 Harlem and 86 Narragansett/Ridgeland run through the Village; 91 Austin runs the eastern edge; 20 Madison, 66 Chicago, 70 Division, and 126 Jackson end at Austin Blvd.
- Schedule: [GTFS](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/gtfs/), zip at `https://www.transitchicago.com/downloads/sch_data/google_transit.zip` (about 100 MB, no key). In the schedule for Wednesday, September 9, 2026: the Blue Line runs all night, 174 trains each way, every 5 to 7 minutes at peak and every 10 midday; the Green Line runs from about 3:45 a.m. to about 1 a.m., 128 trains each way, every 8 minutes at peak and every 10 midday and evening; route 90 runs 4:42 a.m. to 11:16 p.m. every 14 minutes at peak and 20 midday; route 86 runs 5:46 a.m. to 9:45 p.m. every 15 to 19 minutes at peak, 20 midday, 30 in the evening.
- Real time: [Bus Tracker API](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/bustracker/) (key from `https://www.ctabustracker.com/bustime/apps/getapikey.jsp`, a JavaScript form, open it in a browser; [developer guide PDF](https://www.transitchicago.com/assets/1/6/cta_Bus_Tracker_API_Developer_Guide_and_Documentation_2025-04-21.pdf)) and [Train Tracker API](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/traintracker/) ([apply here](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/traintrackerapply/), [docs](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/ttdocs/)). Keys usually arrive within a day or two, so someone should apply before the event; the [Developer Center](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/) is the index. JSON output is available on both.
- Accessibility: the [Customer Alerts API](https://www.transitchicago.com/developers/alerts/) needs no key, and `https://www.transitchicago.com/api/1.0/alerts.aspx?accessibility=true&activeonly=true&outputType=JSON` returns the current elevator outages (60 alerts system-wide when checked, each tagged with the station). The [accessibility status page](https://www.transitchicago.com/travel-information/accessibility-status/) is the same data for humans, and the [List of 'L' Stops](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/CTA-System-Information-List-of-L-Stops/8pix-ypme) dataset carries an `ada` flag per station: true for Harlem/Lake only among the Oak Park stations.
- Ridership: the two portal datasets above, cached. Weekday entries at Harlem/Lake averaged 3,585 in 2019 and 2,173 in 2025; Oak Park (Green) 1,559 and 848; Oak Park (Blue) 1,768 and 602. Route 91 Austin averaged 6,031 weekday riders in 2019 and 4,265 in 2025.

**Pace.** Seven routes have stops in the stops file: 307 Harlem, 309 Lake Street, 311 Oak Park Avenue, 313 St. Charles Road, 314 Ridgeland Avenue, 315 Austin Boulevard (its stops sit on the Chicago side of Austin, at the file's eastern edge), 318 West North Avenue. The file also carries Pace's Spring 2026 passenger counts.
- Schedule: [GTFS page](https://www.pacebus.com/gtfs) with the August 2026 zip at `https://www.pacebus.com/sites/default/files/2026-08/GTFS.zip` (8 MB, no key; the [license](https://www.pacebus.com/route-timetable-data-services) is non-commercial). Same weekday: 307 and 318 run about 5 a.m. to midnight every 15 minutes, 30 in the evening; 309 and 313 run about 5 a.m. to 10 or 11 p.m. every 30 minutes at peak and hourly midday; 311 runs 5:18 a.m. to 10:39 p.m. about every 30; 314 runs 5:41 a.m. to 7:47 p.m. hourly, 15 trips a day each way; 315 runs 5:29 a.m. to 12:44 a.m. every 15 minutes at peak and about every 16 to 18 the rest of the day.
- Real time: Pace publishes no GTFS-realtime feed and no documented API; its data page says plainly that Bus Tracker predictions "are not available for download," and it points riders at the Transit app ([Bus Tracker tools](https://www.pacebus.com/bus-tracker-tools)). The web [Bus Tracker](https://tmweb.pacebus.com/TMWebWatch/) is driven by JSON endpoints under `https://tmweb.pacebus.com/TMWebWatch/Arrivals.aspx/` (POST `getRoutes` with `{}` lists every route with its internal id; 307 is 33, 309 is 35, 311 is 37, 313 is 38, 314 is 271, 315 is 39, 318 is 41; `getDirections` with `{"routeID":33}` returns North and South; `getStops` and `getStopTimes` follow the same pattern). It is undocumented and could change; a demo that reads it should say so on screen.
- Accessibility: every Pace bus is lift or ramp equipped ([riders with disabilities](https://www.pacebus.com/riders-disabilities)). [ADA Paratransit](https://www.pacebus.com/ada) covers trips within three quarters of a mile of a fixed route, at the same hours, for riders certified by NITA, $3.25 a ride booked the day before; the [West Suburban Cook reservation line](https://www.pacebus.com/directory/paratransit-reservation-line-west-suburban-cook-county), (800) 299-0765, lists Oak Park Township in its area and runs Monday to Friday 5 a.m. to 1 a.m., Saturday 7 a.m. to 12:45 a.m., Sunday 8 a.m. to 12:45 a.m. The [Taxi Access Program](https://www.pacebus.com/tap) changed on May 1, 2026. None of the 13 [Cook County Dial-a-Ride programs](https://www.pacebus.com/dial-ride-services-cook-county) includes Oak Park, and no [Pace On Demand](https://www.pacebus.com/ondemand) zone does either.
- Ridership: the stop-level counts in the cached file; route-level history at [RTAMS, Pace ridership by route](https://www.rtams.org/ridership/pace/routes).

**Metra.** One station, Oak Park (Marion Street) on the Union Pacific West line, 1115 W. North Blvd.
- Schedule: [developers page](https://metra.com/developers); GTFS zip at `https://schedules.metrarail.com/gtfs/schedule.zip` (no key, under 1 MB; `https://schedules.metrarail.com/gtfs/published.txt` says when it changed). The station's `stop_id` is `OAKPARK`. Same weekday: 28 trains each way; inbound 5:20 a.m. to 11:30 p.m., outbound 6:01 a.m. to 12:56 a.m.; every 16 to 20 minutes at peak, every 60 to 90 minutes midday.
- Real time: GTFS-realtime `alerts`, `positions`, and `tripupdates` at `https://gtfspublic.metrarr.com/gtfs/public/…` with an `api_token` ([API page](https://metra.com/metra-gtfs-api)); the free key form is on the developers page after the [license](https://metra.com/gtfs-realtime-api-key-request-license-agreement). Protocol Buffers, so the team needs a GTFS-rt decoder (Python `gtfs-realtime-bindings` or the JavaScript one on cdnjs). Metra requires you to proxy the feed rather than call it from a public page.
- Accessibility: the [station page](https://metra.com/train-lines/stations/oak-park) says Accessibility: Yes, one ADA parking space of 208; [Accessibility at Metra](https://metra.com/accessibility) has the system view.
- Ridership: Metra publishes [monthly reports](https://metra.com/ridership-and-on-time-performance) by line, not by station, plus the [fall 2018 boarding and alighting count](https://metra.com/document/2018summaryresultsreportfinalpdf). [RTAMS, Metra ridership by station](https://www.rtams.org/ridership/metra/stations) tabulates the surveys: Oak Park had 960 weekday boardings in 2002, 1,129 in 2014, 905 in 2016, 991 in 2018. Nothing newer per station exists.

**Oak Park Township.** The Township's [Senior Services page](https://oakparktownship.org/senior-services/) describes its transportation program: curb-to-curb rides anywhere in Oak Park and River Forest, plus select hospitals and medical facilities, for residents 60 and over and adults 18 and over with a disability, on lift-equipped buses; book by calling the Transportation Department at (708) 383-4806 at least 24 hours and up to a week ahead, first come first served; suggested donation $1 a ride; seasonal rides to the Farmers' Market. The same page lists taxicab coupon books ($6 for $10 of Blue Cab or 303 Taxi fare, call (708) 383-8005), RTA reduced-fare permits, and temporary disability parking placards. The Township publishes no schedule, no hours for the phone line, and no ridership; that is a gap the team can name. The Village's own [Transportation page](https://www.oak-park.us/Services-Parking/Transportation) (oak-park.us blocks scripts; open it in a browser) links to CTA, Metra, and Pace and lists no Village-run shuttle or taxi subsidy.

**Bikes and everything else.** Divvy has no stations in Oak Park. The GBFS feed (`https://gbfs.divvybikes.com/gbfs/gbfs.json`, which points to `https://gbfs.lyft.com/gbfs/1.1/chi/en/station_information.json`) listed 2,054 stations when checked and zero inside the box `-87.8135, 41.8698, -87.7755, 41.9045`; the nearest are on the Chicago side of Austin Blvd at Lake, Chicago, and Madison, and public racks at North and Roosevelt. The Village [ended its 13-station Divvy contract in January 2018](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2018/01/17/for-whom-the-bike-bell-tolls-oak-park-votes-to-kill-its-divvy-program) and its Bicycling page lists no bike or scooter share today. Amtrak does not stop in Oak Park; the nearest stations are Chicago Union Station and [La Grange Road](https://www.amtrak.com/stations/lag). Pace's [I-290 and I-88 express bus study](https://www.pacebus.com/I-290-I-88-study) names Oak Park as a connection, but no expressway route serves the Village yet. School buses are out of scope.

## First win (15 minutes)

Build the provider table by hand, one row per provider (CTA rail, CTA bus, Pace bus, Metra, Pace ADA paratransit, Township transportation), with six columns: what it runs in Oak Park, first and last service on a weekday, frequency at the morning peak, wheelchair access, where the real-time feed is and what key it needs, and whether any ridership is published. Most cells are in this brief and in `transit-stops-oak-park.csv`; the Pace and Township rows will have "none" in the real-time column, and only Harlem/Lake and Metra get an unqualified yes on access. That table is the opening slide, and it is also the first integrated view anyone at the Village will have seen.

## The build (by 2:15)

A single web page, Leaflet plus `fetch`, that loads `transit-stops-oak-park.csv` and draws every stop and station colored by provider, with the bikeways layer underneath. Clicking a stop opens a panel with the routes, the weekday span and headway from the table, the access flag, and the next arrivals from whichever real-time feed serves it: Train Tracker for the six stations, Bus Tracker for the CTA stops, Metra's `tripupdates` for Oak Park station, the Bus Tracker JSON for Pace (or "no public feed" if the team decides not to depend on it). A small proxy is unavoidable for keyed feeds; a 40-line Python `http.server` or a Cloudflare Worker that adds the key and the CORS header is enough. A "who can ride this" filter at the top toggles among everyone, seniors 60 and over (adds the Township program and the taxi coupons), and ADA certified riders (adds Pace paratransit and the three quarter mile buffer around every fixed route), and the elevator feed turns a station red when its elevator is out. The demo is one address, three filter settings, and a live arrival time.

## Stretch

- Ridership trends: from `cta-ridership-oak-park.csv`, plot weekday entries by station 2015 to 2026 and the five-station recovery curve since 2020; rerun the script with `--since 2001` for the full arc. Put Metra's four survey points and Pace's stop counts on the same page, with the caveats.
- Transit and zoning: what exists and what is permitted within a quarter mile of each station (see the [Transit and Zoning Opportunity Explorer](../project-ideas.md#9-transit-and-zoning-opportunity-explorer) prompt), draws walking-distance buffers around the stations and summarizes zoning inside them. This dashboard is the base map that idea needs.
- A printable "how to get to X without a car" guide: pick five destinations (the hospital, the Main Library, a school, the Farmers' Market, Ogilvie) and generate one page each from the schedule data, with the accessible option called out.
- Service-change diffing: keep this month's Pace and CTA GTFS and rerun the table in a month to see what the overhaul changed.

## No-code roles

- Table owner: fill the provider table from the links above and check every cell against the provider's own page; that table is deliverable number one.
- Rider survey: five riders at the Oak Park Green Line station or the Metra platform. Which app do you use, do you know about the Township buses, has an elevator ever stranded you, what would one page need to show. Their answers set the filter design.
- Township writeup: call (708) 383-4806, ask what the vehicle hours are, how many rides a month they give, and whether they would share a count; write up what the program is and what is not published.
- Demo owner: "here is every provider, here is what is running now, here is who can ride it, and here is what the Village cannot see today."

## Claude tips

Ask for a single-file Leaflet page that loads `transit-stops-oak-park.csv` with `fetch`, colors markers by `agency`, and opens a side panel on click. Paste the Train Tracker docs page and ask for the `fetch` call for `mapid=41350` plus a 40-line Python proxy that adds the key and `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`. Ask for the same for Metra's `tripupdates` feed with a Protocol Buffers decoder and a filter on `stop_id = OAKPARK`. Paste ten rows of `cta-ridership-oak-park.csv` and ask for a weekday-only annual average per station and a chart. Ask it to draw a three quarter mile buffer around every Pace stop in the file and tell you which block groups in `social-vulnerability-oak-park.geojson` fall outside it. For the elevator layer, paste the alerts JSON and ask it to match `ImpactedService.Service[].ServiceId` (the CTA station id) to `stop_id` in the stops file.
